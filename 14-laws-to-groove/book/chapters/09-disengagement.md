# LAW 09 — DISENGAGEMENT

## Understanding the Law

Disengagement is the decision to end your participation in an exchange or situation. It can preserve safety, dignity, attention and the ability to act later. The useful question is what leaving now makes possible and what responsibilities or dangers will remain.

## The Reality Behind It

An argument can continue after it has stopped producing information. If every answer starts another accusation, an additional explanation may accomplish little. Leaving an unsafe relationship involves different stakes. The National Domestic Violence Hotline describes safety planning as individualized and relevant while experiencing abuse, preparing to leave or after leaving. The safest sequence depends on the circumstances. [9]

## Where We Go Wrong

We sometimes stay to prove that we cannot be intimidated. We also sometimes leave because somebody raised an issue we owe them an answer about. A pause can serve both people when it has a purpose and a time to return. There are situations where announcing a departure or setting a follow-up could increase danger. In those circumstances prioritize safety and trusted support.

## Applying the Law

In an ordinary disagreement, state what is happening and what you can do next. “I'm too upset to finish this conversation. I can talk tomorrow afternoon.” Step away and return when you said you would. If the interaction involves harassment at work or school, preserve relevant records and consider which channels can receive them. Where safety is at stake, make a plan with a trusted person or a qualified advocate if you can do so safely.

## The Community Connection

Friends can help someone leave an unsafe setting by listening, respecting their judgment about the risks and asking what practical support they want. Community leaders can create ways to pause or escalate a dispute without requiring the person affected to keep arguing in public.

## The Final Word

You can end a conversation and still take the problem seriously.
