# LAW 07 — ENDOWMENT

## Opening questions

What have people passed down to you that you still use?

How do you usually decide what money to spend and what to set aside?

Who taught you how to handle your first financial decisions?

## Understanding the Law

Endowment is what reaches you and what you make available to somebody else. Property, savings and paid time solve different problems from advice, practical skills, reliable care and professional introductions. A person who can stay with family while taking an unpaid certification course has an advantage even if no money changes hands. A person with a wise mentor can still be short of the cash the landlord requires. Ask what a resource makes possible, when it becomes available and who can decide how it will be used.

Financial net worth is assets minus liabilities. If you own a car worth $12,000 and still owe $9,000 on it, your equity in that car is $3,000. The number matters if you sell, borrow or decide whether you can replace the car. It describes a financial position. Your dignity and the respect you deserve have a different basis. The point of learning the numbers is to understand the choices you have, including the possibility that some decisions should be made collectively.

## The Reality Behind It

**How a dollar circulates.** Suppose a customer pays a local shop $100 for a repair. Part of that price pays for a component shipped from out of state. Part pays rent, and the property owner may live elsewhere. Some pays a technician who spends part of their wages on groceries close to home. The shop may buy bookkeeping from another business nearby. Local circulation describes which of these transactions lead to further spending or income inside a defined area. It depends on the area you draw, what the business buys, where its workers live and what people do with the income.

Owners make decisions about hiring, training, suppliers and retained profits. A locally owned business can rely on a supplier across the country. A business owned elsewhere can employ workers who live nearby. If you want more purchasing to benefit the area, identify a specific barrier, such as a shortage of commercial kitchen space or a contract whose insurance requirement puts it beyond a new vendor's reach.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis explains how regional input-output models estimate the local effects of spending through connected industries. They depend on what firms buy and on the boundary of the study area. A claim that dollars stay within one racial group for a certain number of hours or days needs a traceable study that defines the community and follows purchases, payroll and income. Brookie Madison’s 2015 fact-check for Truth Be Told could not substantiate the widely repeated claim about Black communities. Regional multiplier estimates do not supply that statistic.

**Wealth and the starting line.** The Federal Reserve’s report on the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances, published in 2023, documents large differences in family wealth by racial and ethnic group. The survey compares family balance sheets. It does not track how long a customer’s payment stays in a neighborhood. Assets can pay a deposit, buy time during a job search or secure a loan. Two families with similar incomes can face very different choices when a car breaks down.

The Mapping Inequality archive preserves Home Owners’ Loan Corporation neighborhood maps and descriptions produced in the 1930s. Some descriptions explicitly treated race as a risk. The maps and descriptions document what officials recorded in the 1930s. To determine why a lender denied a particular family a mortgage, look for the application and the lender’s records.

**Investment and displacement.** A neighborhood can benefit from repaired buildings, new shops and better connections to jobs. The benefits depend in part on who can stay long enough to use them. A renter can lose a home after a rent increase or a building sale. An owner may gain equity and still face rising assessments, insurance or repair bills that squeeze monthly cash flow. A local merchant can win customers as the area changes and lose the lease when renewal comes up. The word displacement can refer to several outcomes, including a move a household would not have chosen, the loss of an affordable option for a future resident, or the closing of a workplace people rely on.

Quentin Brummet and Davin Reed’s 2019 working paper studied neighborhood change and original residents in large U.S. cities. It estimated some additional out-migration alongside benefits for some residents who stayed. That combination is worth dwelling on. A neighborhood average can rise while a household loses access to its home. A study following original residents answers different questions from a count of the affordable homes that remain.

## Where We Go Wrong

**All Skinfolk Ain't Kinfolk.** Supporting your community is important. But support should never require you to abandon your standards.

There's a saying in the Black community: *All skinfolk ain't kinfolk.* Sharing a racial identity does not automatically mean sharing the same values, intentions, or respect for others.

A Black-owned business deserves the opportunity to earn your support. It does not automatically deserve your money.

When a customer pays for a service, they're entering an agreement. The business promises something of value, and the customer provides compensation. Both parties have responsibilities. The customer should respect the business's time, policies, and labor. The business should provide the product or service it advertised, communicate honestly, and treat customers professionally.

Too often, conversations about supporting Black businesses stop at the transaction. We encourage people to spend money within the community, but we don't always discuss what happens after that money changes hands.

Did the business deliver what it promised? Was the price explained? Was the customer treated respectfully? If something went wrong, did the owner take responsibility?

These questions matter because economic empowerment requires more than exchanging money. It requires building institutions and relationships that people can trust.

At the same time, we must recognize that a small business may have higher operating costs than a national corporation. A higher price is not automatically evidence of exploitation. Quality materials, fair wages, insurance, rent, and limited purchasing power all affect what a business must charge.

The standard should be fairness, not simply cheapness.

Supporting a business can also mean giving constructive feedback, recommending good service, sharing professional knowledge, or helping an owner identify resources that improve operations.

But loyalty without accountability can become permission for bad behavior.

**Community support should create opportunity, not immunity from responsibility.**

And that principle applies to everyone. A business owner should not exploit customers in the name of community, and customers should not demand free labor or unreasonable discounts in the name of solidarity.

The goal isn't simply to keep money circulating.

It's to build something worth circulating money through.

## Applying the Law

**Budget by pay date.** Put the dates and amounts of take-home pay on a calendar. Next to them, enter rent, utilities, transportation, food, insurance and minimum debt payments. Include expenses that show up irregularly. A $360 annual bill requires saving $30 a month if you have twelve months to prepare. If the bill is due in three months and you have nothing set aside, the immediate number is $120 a month. An annual average is helpful for a plan and can be misleading about cash you need this Thursday.

Imagine two $950 paychecks and a $1,100 rent payment due before the second one. The month’s income covers the rent, but you are $150 short on the day it is due. Budgeting by date lets you prepare from a prior paycheck, ask to move the due date or look for help before a fee is added. A persistent shortfall calls for looking at income, benefits and obligations as well as expenses.

**Give checking and savings different jobs.** Use checking for bills and daily transactions. A savings account can hold an emergency reserve or money assigned to a later expense. Compare overdraft terms, fees, minimum balances, withdrawal access and deposit insurance. The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation explains that the standard limit is generally $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Deposit insurance covers eligible deposits under its rules. Investments can lose value.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s emergency-fund guide starts with the kinds of unplanned expenses you actually face. A first target can be the repair, deductible or income gap you are most likely to have to cover. Keep the money accessible and estimate how long a transfer takes. If a scheduled transfer would cause an overdraft, lower it or move it to a date when the balance is available.

**Understand the report and the score.** The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s guidance distinguishes a credit report, which records information about credit accounts and related activity, from a credit score, which uses a scoring model and data to estimate credit risk. FICO is one family of models. Other models exist, and the same person can receive different scores depending on the model, data and date. Review your reports through the federally authorized service and dispute specific errors with the reporting company and the business that furnished the information. Preserve copies, including the dates you sent them.

Payment history and revolving credit utilization can matter. Utilization compares reported revolving balances with credit limits. A $400 reported balance against a $1,000 limit is 40 percent on that account. Your reported balance can differ from what you owe today. You generally do not need to pay interest to show that you use credit. Check the card’s grace period, especially if you carried a balance from a prior month.

**Borrow with the full cost in view.** On a credit card, paying the minimum may keep the account current and leave principal accruing interest. Compare annual percentage rate, fees and projected payoff time. Consider a $5,000 loan. Even before comparing fees, a longer term can lower the monthly payment while increasing the amount you pay over time. In a mortgage, compare written estimates using the same loan size and term, and check the cash needed at closing, whether the rate can change, and what the payment excludes. A property tax increase can change the cost of owning a home even when mortgage principal and interest stay fixed.

A weak or thin credit file can narrow access to some rentals, mortgages or small-business loans, especially when a lender considers a personal guarantee. Scores are one part of an underwriting decision. Ask what information the lender considers and compare more than one offer. Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and its implementing Regulation B, creditors generally cannot discriminate on the basis of race or other protected characteristics. If you receive an adverse decision, preserve the offer, application and notice. Ask for the specific reasons when you are entitled to them, and consider an appropriate consumer agency or qualified legal adviser if you suspect discrimination. An unexplained denial is a reason to gather evidence, not a conclusion about motive.

**Read the 401(k) plan.** A 401(k) is a U.S. employer-sponsored retirement plan. Ask whether the employer matches contributions, what formula it uses and when your right to keep employer contributions vests. Imagine a plan matching fifty cents for each dollar you contribute, up to six percent of eligible pay. A contribution of six percent would receive a match equal to three percent under that formula. Before leaving a job, check when the match posts and what portion you can keep.

The Internal Revenue Service distinguishes traditional contributions, which generally defer federal income tax until distribution, from designated Roth contributions, which are made after tax and can lead to tax-free qualified distributions. The plan may offer one or both. Examine both contribution choices and investment choices. A contribution puts money in the account. Check what the money buys, what the plan and investments cost, and who your beneficiary is.

**Match investment risk to the job.** The Securities and Exchange Commission’s guide to allocation and diversification explains how time horizon and risk tolerance inform the mix of investments. Diversification can limit the effect of any one company performing badly. It cannot prevent losses in a broad decline. A fund that holds a narrow industry may expose you to more of the same risk even if you also own another fund with a different name. Compare what the funds hold, what they charge and the time you can leave the money invested. Ask how anyone who recommends an investment is paid and what it would cost to get out.

## The Community Connection

**Follow the decision.** Turn a rumor about redevelopment into a claim you can check. Find the address, parcel or application number, and the office with authority. Depending on local rules, a planning commission may recommend a zoning change and an elected body may adopt it. Ask the clerk whether the proposal has reached a vote or still needs permission. Read the notice, staff report, minutes and adopted ordinance. Compare the final text with the proposal.

If public money is involved, look for the budget line, the contract and any conditions on payment. Ask how long promised affordable units will be affordable and who qualifies. Search property records for recorded owners and transfers. Check assessments, tax relief and appeal deadlines with the assessor. If a record is missing, ask the clerk which office holds it and request the project name, document and date range in writing.

Find the hearing and public-comment deadlines. Ask for something the body can decide, such as requiring a relocation plan before approval. Find out where the response will be recorded. A renter, an older owner on a fixed income and a young adult looking for a first apartment may need different protections. Compare proposals by who qualifies, who pays and how long protections last. Include those residents before speaking in the name of the neighborhood.

**Pass on the method.** Teach someone how to read a pay stub, compare a loan estimate or find a planning notice. Use sample records in a workshop and get permission before sharing a family document or making an introduction. A useful lesson shows where the rule comes from, what to do if it changes and who can answer a harder question.

### A Different Kind of Strength

Some people grow up believing that a man's responsibility is to provide financially, protect physically, and remain emotionally strong regardless of what he's experiencing. Protection can also mean helping a child feel safe enough to tell you what happened. A younger brother who is already ashamed may need someone to let him finish a sentence before he can hear any advice.

A man's presence should mean more than occupying a place in the household. Presence is being available, keeping promises and recognizing when someone is struggling. A father doesn't have to know every answer. Admitting he was wrong, apologizing and asking for help can give a child examples to draw on the next time they make a mistake. An older brother, uncle, coach or mentor can offer that kind of care as well.

Consistent, loving, responsible adults matter, and men have a meaningful role in providing that care. A mother, grandparent or another caregiver can give a child dependable care in many different family arrangements. If you grew up without a dependable father, that absence does not decide what you can offer. Learn from the people who earned your trust and ask for guidance where you need it.

Make your care reliable at a scale you can sustain. Leave room in a regular call for the young person to bring up whatever they need to discuss. Ask whether they want you to listen, help them think, or take an action you both understand. If you agree to help with a bill or an application, follow through and explain anything you cannot do. A young person may remember the money you gave them and carry the conversation for the rest of their life.

**Being a strong man isn't only about what you can withstand. It's also about what people can safely bring to you.**

## The Final Word

Measure an inheritance partly by what it makes possible for the person who receives it. A useful endowment gives them resources they can count on, knowledge they can test and room to decide how to use both.
