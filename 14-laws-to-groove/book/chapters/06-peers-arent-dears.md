# LAW 06 — PEERS AREN'T DEARS

## Understanding the Law

Familiarity does not excuse disrespect. A friendship can include teasing, disagreements and hard truths. Its health depends on whether people can object to name-calling, degrading comments or public embarrassment without having to surrender their place in the group.

## The Reality Behind It

An audience changes a remark. When a friend repeats a private disclosure as a joke, the listener has to handle the breach of trust and the question of whether to object in public. A laugh may express amusement or an effort to get through the moment. StopBullying.gov describes power imbalance and repetition or the potential for repetition in its youth bullying definition. That framework helps distinguish a harmful pattern from a single conflict, although a single incident can still demand repair. [6]

## Where We Go Wrong

A group makes disrespect easier when its response is “you know how she is.” The same room may expect the person who was hurt to demonstrate loyalty by staying quiet. The conflict also tests reciprocal standards. If you expect control over information about your own family or money, ask permission before telling a story that relies on a friend's private information.

## Applying the Law

Name the conduct and make a specific request. “Keep my rent situation out of the group chat” gives the other person a clear line. Decide what you will do if it happens again. Reducing contact and limiting what you share are decisions available to you. When you are the one who crossed a line, hear the account, say where the remark traveled and ask what a correction should contain. Repeating a secret to explain an apology can spread the harm.

## The Community Connection

Friends can interrupt a pile-on without repeating the insult. They can call the person affected privately and leave them room to decide what happens next. Schools, mentorship groups and teams can make an independent reporting route available when a leader takes part in the conduct.

## The Final Word

There are people who can hear “that hurt” and keep treating you as a friend. Pay attention to how they respond.
