# A Word from the Author

**JABARI JAJA**

I've always believed that understanding life requires more than simply experiencing it. You have to pay attention to what happens around you, question what you're told, examine your own decisions, and be willing to learn from outcomes that don't go according to plan.

That's how I approach life.

I observe before I act. I research what I don't understand. I test ideas, consider different perspectives, and develop plans before committing to a direction. Information can be incomplete, circumstances can change, and obstacles can arise that no preparation could have prevented.

I've learned that preparation doesn't eliminate uncertainty. It gives you a better way to respond to it.

The fourteen laws in this book reflect principles I use to navigate those situations. Reflection reminds me to examine my own actions before judging someone else's. Scout encourages me to understand an environment before entering it. I Say = I Will challenges me to honor my commitments. Risky Rewards teaches me to consider what I stand to gain alongside what I can afford to lose. Strategy and Planning help me turn ideas into decisions I can act on.

And when circumstances become difficult, Disengagement reminds me that walking away can be just as important as knowing when to continue.

These laws aren't a claim that I have mastered life. They're a framework I return to as I continue learning, building, making mistakes, and adjusting my approach. They've helped me think more carefully about my identity, my relationships, and the responsibilities that come with the opportunities I pursue.

I also recognize that effort alone doesn't determine every outcome. Access, resources, relationships, and circumstances can create obstacles that individuals cannot overcome through discipline alone. Understanding those realities is part of making informed decisions, not an excuse to stop trying.

I wrote *The 14 Laws to Groove* because I believe useful knowledge should be shared. A lesson that helps one person recognize a mistake, avoid an unnecessary risk, strengthen a relationship, or create an opportunity deserves to reach someone else.

I hope readers question these laws, test them against their own experiences, and discover what they can contribute to the people and communities around them.

**I'm still learning how to Groove. These are the lessons I'm bringing with me.**

**— Jabari Jaja**
