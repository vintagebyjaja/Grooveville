# Conclusion

The laws in this book ask you to notice two things at once: the choice available to you and the conditions under which you have to make it. Each can change. A person who could barely get a question answered in one room may be responsible for hearing everybody's questions in another. A young worker who needs the last bus home may one day set the shift schedule. What you learn about an institution when you have little authority can become part of how you use authority later.

Some laws teach a habit that you can practice by yourself. You can put your actual travel time on a calendar. You can review the budget before agreeing to a monthly payment. You can ask what evidence supports a judgment. Other practices depend on people changing the way they work together. A manager can publish the requirements for a promotion. A group can make a trusted route for reporting mistreatment. A family can explain which help it can provide. Pay attention to the moment when the problem shifts from how you prepare to how the people around you share responsibility.

There will be times when the laws pull in different directions. Reliability may call for staying to finish a job you agreed to do. Mental Awareness may tell you the pace is hurting you. Strategy asks whether you can obtain relief, arrange a handoff or change the schedule. Reflection asks why your first impulse is to hide the problem. Treat those questions as part of the decision. Your obligation to another person may be real, and you may need to change how you meet it.

Consider the role that respect plays in these decisions. You can speak with confidence after you learn to recognize your own worth. You can also be uncertain, tired or new to a place and deserve fair treatment. Give other people that same consideration. In an institution, it can mean writing down what somebody actually did before judging their character. In a friendship, it can mean hearing a boundary without making the other person defend their right to one.

Put the book down with a particular decision in mind. Choose one promise to clarify, one conversation to give your full attention, or one opportunity whose terms you can check. Choose something you can act on this week. Then notice what changed for the person who had to live with the result, including yourself. Over time, that feedback can help you build a life you can recognize from the inside.
